# Observation Log — Manual Nudge Field Test

**What this is:** raw notes from a 4-day (1–4 Sept 2026), 6-person manual WhatsApp nudge experiment. No app was built, nothing was spent. Every nudge was typed and sent by hand; every reply was logged as it came in. This log includes what was changed mid-test and what happened immediately after each change.

**What was exported:** the full WhatsApp chat history for all 6 participants (Archana, Karishma, Santosh, Soumya, Sushant, Vandita), and a hand-maintained spreadsheet log (`Date, Name, Focused work time, Start/End work time, Nudge #, Nudge time, Acted Y/N, Notes`). Total: 124 individual nudges sent and classified.

---

## Day 1 (1 Sept) — Raw observations

- Onboarding messages sent to all 6 the morning of. Each confirmed their start time, deep-focus window(s), and logout time by reply.
- **Karishma, 12:16 PM:** first nudge sent. Reply, same minute: *"I have back to back calls till 1pm."* Logged as missed.
- **Karishma, 1:05 PM:** catch-up nudge sent assuming calls had ended. No immediate reply.
- **Karishma, 2:48 PM (delayed):** she confirms, *"No I was continuously sitting on a call."* — the catch-up also failed, for the same reason as the first.
- **Change made:** starting later that same day, on-call-friendly seated stretch suggestions (neck rolls, shoulder shrugs, seated twist) were added into her nudges as a fallback line, specifically because the standard "step away" framing had now failed twice for the identical reason.
- **What happened after the change:** her next three nudges that day (4:03 PM, 5:40 PM) were both confirmed "Done." No way to isolate whether the seated-stretch framing itself caused this, or she was simply off calls by then — noted as an open question, not a confirmed causal link.
- **Vandita, 7:24–7:28 PM:** unprompted, she volunteered pushback on the format itself: *"It's difficult to clock in each and every minute... I take a break when I feel like it, instead of waiting for some application to tell me that break lelo."* This was said even as she gave a batch 👍 confirming she'd followed the day's nudges — noted as a contradiction worth watching (compliant in behavior, resistant in stated preference).
- **Archana:** the only participant with zero individually-confirmed nudges all day; her only signal was a late (9:42 PM) batch message saying 2 of the day's nudges (unspecified which) were followed.
- Sushant's last nudge of the day (10:36 PM) was initially declined ("No," "Leaving for the day"), then reframed by the tester as "walk to the cab" counting as movement — he agreed ("Yes"). Noted as an example of a human tester improvising in the moment; a rule-based system would not have made this judgment call automatically.

## Day 2 (2 Sept) — Raw observations

- **Change carried forward:** the seated/on-call stretch fallback, introduced for Karishma on Day 1, was used again for her at 12:18 PM and thereafter — she confirmed "Done" on 3 of her 5 nudges that day, a marked improvement over Day 1's near-total failure on her first two.
- **Vandita:** compliance dropped — a Day 2 batch note reads "(Followed 3 nudges)" out of 5 sent, first sign of the decline her Day 1 comment predicted.
- **Sushant:** two nudges (1:38 PM, 3:33 PM) drew no reply at all — first appearance of a pattern (silence rather than explicit decline) that recurred more on Day 3.
- **Soumya:** explicitly declined one nudge at 5:28 PM, replying hours later (7:59 PM), *"No was into continuous meetings"* — a second live instance of the Context Lock-Out theme, independent of Karishma's.
- **Observation, not yet acted on:** by end of Day 2, two participants (Vandita, and Archana still near-silent) were showing early disengagement; no format change was made for either yet — held off to avoid over-reacting to two days of data.

## Day 3 (3 Sept) — Raw observations

- This was the sharpest drop in the whole test.
- **Karishma:** zero nudges confirmed all day. At 11:28 PM she explained, unprompted: *"I had a very busy day today. Couldn't follow the nudges."*
- **Archana:** zero nudges confirmed all day, and no reply at all to a direct 9:22 PM check-in asking if any had been followed.
- **Vandita:** zero nudges confirmed all day, also no reply to a direct check-in.
- **Change made, effective immediately (going into Day 4):** for Vandita specifically, nudge volume was cut — the tester decided not to keep sending a full day's worth of nudges into confirmed silence. This was a direct, human, in-the-moment decision, not a rule triggered automatically.
- **What happened after the change:** on Day 4, only one combined check-in/nudge was sent to Vandita all day (down from 5). It also drew no reply. The test was then formally discontinued for her — logged as "(Stopped sending nudges as no response)."
- **Cross-check:** Sushant and Soumya, who had no external workload complaint that day, both still dipped (Sushant to 1 of 6 confirmed; Soumya held up better at 5 of 6) — suggesting the Day 3 dip wasn't purely a per-person workload story; something about that specific day (a Thursday) affected the group as a whole, worth further investigation rather than a settled conclusion.

## Day 4 (4 Sept) — Raw observations

- **Archana:** explained her pattern for the first time, mid-morning: *"Yes I have release on Monday so have not been able to check msgs so missed the reminders... The only thing is the phone should be around. I sometimes keep my phone away unconsciously."* This reframed her whole 4-day low-compliance record — not disinterest, but a channel-access constraint (phone not physically on hand), which a background app notification could solve differently than a WhatsApp message requiring her to be actively on her phone.
- **Karishma:** partial rebound (2 of 6 confirmed before a 9:51 PM message: *"Sure rishu sorry I'm extremely busy with lots of things around and festival"*) — a second, different external cause (festival prep) than Day 3's "busy day."
- **Santosh, Soumya:** both closed the test at or near their Day 1 levels (Santosh 4 of 5 confirmed via end-of-day batch; Soumya 4 of 4) — no format changes were needed for either across all 4 days.
- **Sushant:** last nudge of the test (9:49 PM) confirmed "Already done" — closed on a high note after Day 3's dip.
- Exit questions sent to all still-engaged participants at end of Day 4; several replies still pending logging at time of this write-up (noted, not fabricated).

## Summary of what changed, and what happened after, across the test

| Change made | Trigger | What happened after |
|---|---|---|
| Seated/on-call stretch fallback added for Karishma | Two identical call-blocked failures on Day 1 | Improved compliance for her over the following ~2 days (not proven causal, but directly followed) |
| Reduced nudge frequency for Vandita | Explicit Day 0 pushback + declining Day 1→3 compliance | Compliance did not recover; test was discontinued for her by Day 4 — the reduction did not reverse disengagement once it had already set in |
| Nudges continued at full volume for Archana despite low replies | No clear cause known yet on Days 1–3 | Day 4 explanation (phone not on hand) arrived only once asked directly — a lesson that low compliance without a stated reason should be asked about sooner, not assumed |
| No change made to Santosh or Soumya's cadence at any point | Sustained high, stable compliance | Both closed the test at 80%+ overall — used as the "no-change control" against which the other changes can be compared |

## Exit Feedback — What Participants Said

5 of 6 participants answered the exit questionnaire (Vandita did not respond — itself consistent with her stated preference not to be chased for check-ins, and treated as data rather than a gap to fill in).

### Raw responses, by person

**Archana** — Usefulness: 5/5
- Still reading/acting on nudges by the end: *"Yes I did check notifications."*
- Change vs. a normal week: *"Yes. This was helpful."*
- Naggy or helpful: *"No (for naggy)."*
- Would want it automatic: *"Yes. The only thing is the phone should be around. I sometimes keep my phone away unconsciously."*

**Sushant** — Usefulness: 4/5
- Still reading/acting: *"Still reading nudges."*
- Change vs. a normal week: *"A little change. Mostly acting on notification of drinking water and taking walk."*
- Naggy or helpful: *"Mostly it is fine. It doesn't disturb as of now."*
- Would want it automatic: *"May be yes but not in weekends."*

**Santosh** — Usefulness: 3.5/5
- Still reading/acting: *"I was still reading it but not with the same interest. I did some time but not every time as I was busy."*
- Change vs. a normal week: *"Not much changes but these were good reminders to look at your self care being at work (it's just 4 days)."*
- Naggy or helpful: *"Not naggy but sometimes feels annoying when you are super busy."*
- Would want it automatic: *"It would be better if it is in the app."*
- Comment: *"Overall these were the good reminders and did help during the times I was not very occupied."*

**Soumya** — Usefulness: 3.5/5
- Still reading/acting: *"I started to tuning them like why no much nudges today"* — a notably different failure mode: she noticed and questioned a *drop* in nudges, not the nudges themselves.
- Change vs. a normal week: *"Yes, I usually did not take break for every 1 or 2 hours in deep focus time"* — implying the nudges did shift behaviour during focus stretches.
- Naggy or helpful: *"Feel too many nudges in deep focus time would be naggy as our concentration will be distracted. Also just a suggestion — usually when our eyes feel strain we normally look out somewhere outside or just getup or change the sitting position."* — a direct product suggestion: detect a natural cue instead of a fixed interval.
- Would want it automatic: *"May be yes if we are really worried about health or any major health issues... in that perspective automatic app should be helpful."* — conditional on personal health motivation, not a given.

**Karishma** — Usefulness: 5/5
- Still reading/acting: *"Yes, I was still reading and acting on your nudges. They were a good reminder and helped me stay mindful about taking breaks."*
- Change vs. a normal week: *"Yes, definitely. I found myself getting up and taking short breaks more often than I normally would."*
- Naggy or helpful: *"Not at all. It felt more like a helpful heads-up rather than being naggy or like someone was checking up on me. The timing and tone made it easy to follow."*
- Would want it automatic: *"Yes, I would definitely consider using it as an automatic app... especially when I'm focused on work and lose track of time."*
- Comment: *"The key would be keeping the reminders simple and not too frequent."*

**Vandita** — No response to the exit questionnaire. Her last logged message in the test itself was the Day 0 pushback (*"I take a break when I feel like it, instead of waiting for some application to tell me"*), and her compliance had already dropped to 0% by Day 3. Her silence on the exit survey is read as a continuation of the same signal, not a missing data point to chase.

### What the feedback confirms or adds, beyond the compliance numbers

- **Average usefulness across the 5 respondents: 4.2 / 5** — notably, this does not track cleanly with compliance. Archana rated it 5/5 despite the lowest compliance (19%) of anyone in the test; Santosh and Soumya rated it lowest (3.5/5) despite having some of the highest compliance. Perceived value and actual follow-through are separate things, not proxies for each other.
- **"Naggy" was reported as situational, not fixed** — Santosh's "not naggy but sometimes feels annoying when you are super busy" and Soumya's "too many nudges in deep focus time would be naggy" both tie the naggy feeling to *workload at the moment*, not to the nudges' tone or wording. This sharpens Theme 3: the risk isn't only phrasing, it's timing relative to how busy someone already feels.
- **Soumya's suggestion is a genuine, user-sourced product idea**: detect a natural cue (eyes drifting, a change in sitting position) instead of firing on a fixed interval. This wasn't asked for — she volunteered it — and it points toward a future, more context-sensitive version of the sitting-duration timer (component 1 in the AI Scope Statement), beyond just the calendar/call check already planned.
- **Delivery channel came up unprompted, twice**: Santosh said it "would be better if it is in the app," and Archana's low compliance traced back to her phone not being physically on hand — both independently pointing at the same limitation of a WhatsApp-based v1, already flagged as a Platform Limitation in the PRD.
- **"Would you want it automatic" answers were conditional, not blanket yeses** — Sushant excluded weekends, Soumya conditioned it on having a personal health motivation, Santosh wanted it moved into an app rather than a texting-style channel. Only Karishma and Archana gave an unqualified yes. This suggests the real answer to "do people want this automated" is "yes, with conditions attached per person" — worth carrying into any adoption/opt-in design, not just building one version for everyone.
- **Vandita's non-response is itself the cleanest confirmation of Theme 3** in the whole test: she is the one participant who told the product, in her own words, exactly what she wouldn't tolerate — and then behaviourally and now even at the feedback stage, she didn't.
