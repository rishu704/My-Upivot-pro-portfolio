### Interview A — Rohan Verma (SDE, Bangalore, WFH)

  * Quote: " Delivery times. Zomato has become... unreliable. Especially evenings. Like yesterday — I ordered biryani at 8:30 PM. App promised 30 minutes. It arrived at 9:45 PM. Cold."

    * Signals: Points out to the longer delay times in delivering the order specially in evenings which even we saw the pattern in Bangalore on the Quick Bites data which we had.

  * Quote: " When Gold was 200 rupees for unlimited free delivery, no restrictions, it was a no-brainer. Now they've added minimum order values, restricted restaurants, handling fees Gold doesn't cover... my monthly savings dropped from about 500 rupees to 150. Membership was 200. Math didn't work anymore."

    * Signals: Users are losing interest in Gold membership as Zomato has introduced so much restrictions like minimum order value, limited restaurants and handling fees which has resulted in savings below the membership cost.

  * Quote: "100 percent. I want to use Zomato. Better restaurant selection than Swiggy in my area. Better UI. But if I keep getting cold food at 10 PM... I'm out."

    * Signals: Rohan hasn't rejected Zomato as a product but he has rejected Zomato's reliability during peak evening hours. Despite preferring Zomato's restaurant selection and UI, repeated late deliveries have forced him to switch to Swiggy.

  * Quote: " Match the ETA to reality. I'm not asking for magic. If the delivery is going to take 60 minutes, tell me 60. Don't promise 30 and give me cold food at 75. Just be honest about the promise."

    * Signals: ETA inaccuracy and late deliveries are major factors for customer dissatisfaction and refunds . Even the Quick Bites data , I can see 16/42 orders which got refunded were due to cold food , late delivery .

### Interview B — Meera Krishnan (Restaurant owner, Chennai)

  * Quote: " We cancel. As many as we can. Maybe 20-30 percent of weekend orders. Sometimes more."

    * Signals: Weekend order cancellations are a major restaurant-side issue, with restaurants reporting cancellations of 20–30% or more of weekend orders.

  * Quote: " Their account manager comes once every 2-3 months. I've told him five times. Nothing changes. The app just gives me one option — mark yourself offline. But then my ratings drop because I've been "unavailable" too much."

    * Signals: Repeated restaurant complaints are not being resolved which force restaurants to go offline to manage capacity, which can negatively impact their ratings.

  * Quote: "100 percent. I'd rather take 5 delivery orders per hour and deliver ALL 5 in 30 minutes, than accept 15 and cancel 10. Same total delivered, way better experience for those 5 users, and I don't get the "low fulfilment" penalty."

    * Signals: Restaurants want greater control over the number of orders they receive during high-demand periods, so they can accept only what they can fulfil and reduce cancellations and fulfilment penalties.

### Interview C — Priya Nair (Marketing exec, Mumbai)

  * Quote: " I'll be scrolling Zomato, find a nice-looking dal makhani, 200 rupees. Add to cart. Go to checkout. See: Total 257 rupees. Delivery fee 45. GST 12. Suddenly it's not 200. It's basically 260. That's almost 30 percent more than I thought I was paying."

    * Signals: Users experience more price at checkout when additional fees significantly increase the final price compared with the item price they initially saw.

  * Quote: " I don't know exactly. It's just when the delivery fee starts feeling like the price of a separate meal. 40 rupees is a vada pav. Two of them, even. Why am I paying vada-pav-price for someone to bring my food?"

    * Signals: Customers think high delivery fees as poor value when the fee becomes comparable to the price of a meal which discourages them from completing the order.

  * Quote: " Show me the total price on the restaurant page. Include delivery. Don't surprise me at the end. Or better — bundle it. "This meal, all-in, is 245 rupees." I decide once."

    * Signals: Customers want to see the total all-inclusive price so they can decide whether to place the order without unexpected charges at checkout.

  * Quote: " I looked at it. 300 rupees for 3 months, or 1200 for a year. My friend Karthik told me the value has dropped a lot in the past few months. Restrictions, minimums. I don't order enough to make even 300 worth it. Maybe if I ordered 15 times a month. I do 5.”

    * Signals: Gold membership feels less valuable to users because of added restrictions, and it may not be worth the cost for customers who don't order frequently.

### Interview D — Karthik Reddy (PM, Bangalore, ex-Gold)

  * Quote: " In July, they added "restrictions". Suddenly it was "Gold-eligible restaurants only" — they cut the eligible list by like 40 percent. Then minimum order value went up from 199 to 249. Then they added a "handling fee" that Gold didn't cover. Then peak-hour surcharges Gold didn't cover either. Layer after layer. "

    * Signals:  Users feel Gold is less useful because fewer restaurants are eligible, minimum order values have increased, and extra fees are not covered.

  * Quote: " No. Just a "sorry to see you go" email. Standard. "

    * Signals: Customers are not given an opportunity to share why they cancelled Gold, which means Zomato may miss important customer pain points.

  * Quote: " If they'd said "Gold is going up to 400 rupees, here's why, here's the new value structure" — I might've paid it if the new value was clear. But hiding changes behind fine print? That's a trust break. I'll never be a full-price advocate for them again."

    * Signals: Customers need clear and transparent communication when Gold pricing or benefits change so they can decide whether the membership is still worth it.

  * Quote: " Actually yes. Swiggy One is 599 for 3 months but the coverage is broader. Testing it. If it holds, I'll shift more of my orders. My data lock-in with Zomato is real — 6 years of order history — but not infinite."

    * Signals: Customers are willing to explore competitors if they offer better benefits and value, even after using Zomato for such a long time.

### Interview E — Anjali Patel (Consultant, Delhi, one-time user)

  * Quote: " Complete disaster. The rider called me 4 times because he couldn't find my building. I sent him the pin location twice. He still couldn't. Finally arrived 65 minutes late - the app had promised 35, it took over an hour. Food was cold. Spring rolls soggy. And the dessert was missing entirely."

    * Signals: The user's first order was badly affected by delivery and navigation problems, resulting in a late delivery, cold food, soggy items, and a missing dessert.

  * Quote: " Probably not. It's not about the discount. It's about the trust. They lost me on day one. First orders should be sacred — like a job interview. You get one shot. And they blew it. "

    * Signals: A bad first-order experience can cause customers to lose trust in the platform, and discounts may not be enough to win them back.

  * Quote: " Look — I don't know how the algorithm decides which rider gets which order. But if I'm a first-time customer, I should probably get the BEST rider, the fastest kitchen, the most reliable route. Instead, I got what felt like the worst. If Zomato knew I was a first-timer, they should have treated me like gold. Instead, they treated me like a rounding error."

    * Signals: First-time customers expect a reliable and smooth first-order experience, as they feel their first order should receive extra attention from the platform which will help them in building trust as well.

  * Quote: " Never. Not one email. Not one push notification. Just silence. Which... also tells me something. If I'm gone and they don't care to know why, I'm not going to care about coming back."

    * Signals: Customers expect the company to acknowledge and follow up after a poor first experience; silence can further damage trust and reduce retention.

### Interview F — Vikram Iyer (Tech Lead, Bangalore, 5-year power user)

  * Quote: " Actually most of Bangalore. Especially between 7 and 9 PM. But Whitefield especially — highest concentration of tech workers ordering dinner, hasn't been matched with delivery supply. The problem is compounding. "

    * Signals: Delivery delays are a major problem during peak hours, especially between 7–9 PM in high-demand areas like Whitefield, where delivery supply is not matching customer demand.

  * Quote: " Restaurant availability on weekends. My favourite South Indian place — Karnataka Kitchen down south, and a similar one here in Bangalore called Kappa's — they're basically offline Sunday afternoons now. Used to be reliable. Now marked 'temporarily closed' every Sunday 12-3."

    * Signals: Restaurants that were previously available are now regularly going offline during Sunday afternoons, reducing customer choice on weekends.

  * Quote: " Gold subscription — I cancelled 3 months ago. Same reason as everyone else. Value dropped without warning."

    * Signals: Customers are losing interest in Gold because they no longer feel they are getting enough value from the membership.

  * Quote: " Sad but honest. And I'm not sure new users have that same stickiness. My cousin joined Zomato last month for the first time. Terrible first delivery experience — cold food, late, rider couldn't find him. Uninstalled within a week. If I were new to Zomato today, I'd probably not stay either."

    * Signals: Customer is telling about the preferences which Zomato remembers from his old order history and also pointed the fact that a poor first-order experience can cause new customers to quickly lose trust and leave the platform.

  * Quote: " "I've thought about this. Two things. First — fix the evening delivery times in high-density tech corridors. Whitefield, Koramangala, HSR, Indiranagar. That's your bleeding wound. Second — protect the first-order experience like your life depends on it. Because for new users, it does.”

    * Signals: Customers want Zomato to fix peak-hour delivery delays in high-demand areas and give extra attention to first-time orders to ensure a smooth and reliable first experience.

## Section 6 — Chosen Problem + JTBD

Peak-hour (7-9 PM) delivery lateness

### My chosen problem

When customers order dinner between 7–9 PM in high-demand areas, they want their food delivered close to the promised timeline but Quick Bites delivery reliability collapses during this exact window evidenced by 66.5% of 7–9 PM orders arriving 15+ minutes late and Vikram's also mentioned that Whitefield's tech employee’s dinner demand hasn't been matched with delivery supply and even Rohan's 75-minute-late cold biryani."

### Why this one?

This problem has the broadest reach among all the problem statements that falls under the SOLVE NOW quadrant. This issue affects the most orders — roughly half of all evening orders — and has the biggest visible drop in customer ratings (4.39 to 2.67) once delivery is late. It's also the most strongly triangulated finding, confirmed by 3 of 6 interviews and Vikram even explains the exact cause i.e. not enough riders for peak dinner demand. This one can realistically be improved in 8-12 weeks through rider capacity increase and ETA-accuracy changes, without needing new infrastructure first.

### JTBD Statement

Structure: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]."

"When I am ordering dinner around 7-9 pm in high demand area, I want to get the close estimated time when my order will be delivered, so I can plan my evening without thinking if my order will be delayed."

### User Story Version

Structure: "As a [persona], I want to [action], so that [benefit]."

"As a working professional ordering my dinner during peak evening time, I want to receive an accurate delivery update, so that I can rely on Quick Bites for my evening meal without frustration."

### Notes on JTBD vs User Story

Optional — 1-2 sentences on why the JTBD format captures something the User Story format misses (e.g. situation-based trigger vs identity-based).

JTBD format focuses on situation – ordering between 7-9 pm window – which also matches the data when the delay in order delivery time is observed without referring to any particular type of customer.

The User Story format is built around one persona which narrows down issue to only one kind of person, when in reality anyone ordering in that window — student, professional, or otherwise — hits the same delay.

So JTBD captures the real trigger i.e. the time and context, while User Story frames it around an identity that isn't actually what's causing the pain.
